How the controls are shared

Rate, Depth and Lo-Fi sit across the top and do one job each. The Tweak knob edits whichever of three parameters its toggle points at, the Tune knob does the same for three more, and holding the Bypass switch swaps both knobs onto a second set of three — the lo-fi parameters.

Tweak — Shape, Div, Type

“To adjust the main parameters, simply move the toggle below the knob to select the parameter you want to control, then use the Tweak knob to dial in the preferred Shape, Tap Division, or program Type.” (sheet 4)

Enclosure print: SHAPE | DIV | TYPE, and beneath it ENV · DRIVE · SPACE

Tune — Tone, Sym, X

The Tune switch works the same way: “move the toggle below the knob to select the parameter you want to control, then use the Tune knob to dial in the preferred Tone, Symmetry, or X.” (sheet 5) X “controls a different effect depending on which program you are using.” (sheet 6)

Enclosure print: TONE | SYM | X, and beneath it AGE · NOISE · WARBLE

Hold Bypass for the lo-fi layer

With the pedal on, “move the toggle below the knob to select the lo-fi parameter you want to modify and adjust the Tweak knob while holding down the Bypass switch.” (sheet 4) The three Tweak positions reach Env, Drive, Space and the three Tune positions reach Age, Noise, Warble, in the same left-centre-right order the enclosure prints them.

“If you aren’t hearing any effect from the lo-fi parameters, check to make sure the main lo-fi knob is turned up as it serves as the master level knob for all lo-fi parameters.” (sheet 4)

A | B | C selects the preset bank

“The M1 offers three banks for storing presets. Each bank holds three presets for a total of nine that you can access without using MIDI.” (sheet 7) Moving the switch recalls “the first (red) preset in that bank”; both stomps together load green, then blue, then red again. (sheet 10)

Not in the manual — how the drawn knobs differ from real ones

On the pedal, the Tweak and Tune pots stay where they are when you move a toggle. The knob is then pointing at a position the newly selected parameter is not set to, and it stays wrong until you turn the knob. What the manual gives you instead is the Preset LED: “When a knob is turned, this LED turns purple to indicate the preset has been modified. When that knob crosses its saved position, the LED changes back to the color of the currently selected preset to let you know its last saved position.” (sheet 7)

The panel above does what a pot cannot: it moves the drawn knob to the selected parameter's value the moment you move the toggle, so that you can read the value off the panel.

Shape and symmetry

Shape picks sine, triangle or square, and Sym deforms the shape you picked. “Setting the Tune knob at noon will yield traditional LFO shapes corresponding to the selected shape. As you move this control away from noon, the LFO wave shape will asymmetrically deform toward the beginning of the wave below noon and toward the end of the wave above noon. For example, selecting the triangle shape and setting the symmetry knob to minimum would produce a traditional sawtooth wave.” (sheet 6)

This curve is drawn from the words on sheet 6, which prints its own pictures of the three waves at minimum and maximum Sym. What the wave looks like at the settings in between is not printed anywhere, so those shapes here are this page's reading of the description. The dashed line is the same shape with Sym at noon.

Shape, Sym and Rate here are the same values as the panel above. The firmware 2.0 manual prints no rate range; the frequency shown is scaled to the 0.2–10 Hz the MKII manual gives for its Rate parameter, so read it as an indication and not as a figure Walrus publish for this pedal. Warble is drawn illustratively: sheet 8 says to “Set the knob below noon for a more gentle ‘warping’ of the wave and above noon to push the LFO into more of a random shape”, and prints no pictures of the result.

Div — the tap division

“Set the switch to the Div position and use the Tweak knob to adjust the tap division used by the M1 to set the LFO speed when tapping a tempo. Choose between quarter, quarter note triplet and eighth note.” (sheet 4) The MKII manual adds dotted eighth and sixteenth to that list.

Shape in Vinyl and Tape vibrato

“Sine will be a standard sine wave, but triangle and square will be curvy variations of a sine wave instead of their usual shape. This is to mimic different types of vinyl and tape motor wear.” (sheet 15)

Shape and Sym in Pattern Tremolo

Tremolo Type 3 gives both knobs a new job. “Use the Shape knob to select one of three pattern groups.” “Use the Sym knob to select one of five patterns in the selected group.” (sheet 15)

The six programs

Each program holds three types and gives the X parameter a different job. Four of the six — Tremolo, Vibrato, Rotary and Filter — also change what one or more of the other knobs do. Click a program to load it into the panel above.

Not in the manual — what moves the filter cutoff

In the Filter program, firmware 2.0 puts the cutoff frequency on the Rate knob and the envelope amount on the Depth knob. Those are the two knobs that set the main LFO's speed and amplitude in every other program. Sheet 17 then does not say whether the LFO still sweeps the cutoff, or what sets its speed and amplitude if it does. The launch-firmware manual told you to “Drive the filter frequency with the main LFO”, and that sentence is not in the 2.0 manual, so this page does not repeat it.

The six lo-fi parameters

Each is set by holding Bypass and turning Tweak or Tune. The Lo-Fi knob then sets how much of all six you hear: “Turning this knob up brings in any lo-fi parameters that are engaged while maintaining their relative mix, making it easy to blend in unique combinations of these effects to taste. Setting this knob to minimum turns off all lo-fi parameters no matter their individual levels.” (sheet 8)

Presets

“The first 9 presets are accessible from the pedal using the Bank switch and both stomp switches. All 128 are accessible via MIDI Program Change messages, which are outlined in the MIDI section.” (sheet 10) The nine come in three banks of three, coloured red, green and blue: “Bank A is presets 0-2, Bank B is 3-5, and Bank C is 6-8.” (sheet 7) Those are the same numbers the program change table on sheet 13 uses.

Recalling one

Set the A|B|C switch to the bank you want. “The pedal will automatically recall the first (red) preset in that bank.” From there, “scroll through the presets in that bank by pressing both stomp switches at the same time. The pedal will load the green preset. Each press of both stomp switches will load the next preset until you get back to red and the pattern repeats inside the selected bank.” (sheet 10)

Saving one

Scroll to the slot you want to overwrite and dial the sound in: “The Rate LED will turn purple indicating the preset has been modified.” Then “hold down the Bypass and Tap switches until the preset LED blinks. The preset is now saved and the LED will return to the preset color.” (sheet 10)

The Rate LED and the Preset LED are one lamp

The diagram on sheet 3 labels it Rate LED; the controls section on sheet 7 calls it the Preset LED, and sheet 10 uses both names for it. It sits above the Tap/Skip switch: “The Preset LED lights up the color of the selected preset (red, green, or blue) and indicates the rate of the LFO by blinking at the current rate.” (sheet 7)

Finding a saved value again

Once the LED is purple, sweep the knob you moved until the LED returns to the preset colour: “This is helpful when you want to tweak a parameter on a preset but cannot remember where a knob was last saved.” (sheet 7)

Everything you hold a switch for

The M1 has no screen. Everything it does beyond turning a knob or flipping a toggle is done by holding a switch down. Three of these are done while power is applied, so you unplug the pedal to reach them.

Global preferences

Bypass mode

“In True Bypass mode, the M1 uses relays to completely bypass all pedal circuitry. In DSP Bypass mode, the M1 locks the relays on and uses the DSP to bypass the pedal. The M1 ships in True Bypass mode by default and will remember the selected bypass mode and use that each time it is powered up until you change it.” (sheet 11)

Hold Bypass while applying power (about two seconds) → Tap scrolls the colour → red is True, blue is DSP → both stomps to store.

Output volume

Hold Bypass until the Bypass LED flashes, keep holding, and use the Depth knob: “Minimum is 0dB, noon is +2dB, maximum is +4dB.”

“Some modulation effects work well at unity gain while others can benefit from a slight increase in volume to help them stand out. If you experience clipping, try lowering the level with this control.” (sheet 11)

Factory reset

To return the pedal to its factory settings, “hold both stomp switches while applying power to the pedal for longer than 10 seconds. After 10 or more seconds, release both stomp switches and the pedal will be reset to factory default settings.” (sheet 11)

MIDI channel

The M1 ships on channel 1. “Hold down both stomp switches at power-up until the Red preset led starts flashing, then release both switches.” Send a Program Change on the channel you want, and “The pedal will save that MIDI channel and only respond to messages on that channel until the user changes it again.” (sheet 12)

MIDI clock

“The M1 accepts MIDI clock and sets its modulation time anytime it sees a change in MIDI clock tempo. MIDI clock, when sent, will override the tempo set with the Time knob or Tap switch. You can, however, tap a new tempo after the tempo has been set with MIDI clock.” Walrus also advise limiting the clock “to only send a few clock pulses at a time since the M1 will quickly lock in the tempo.” (sheet 12)

This page's reading: the M1 has no knob called Time — the knob that sets LFO speed is Rate. “Time” appears once in the whole manual, in that sentence, and the same sentence appears in the manual for the Walrus D1 delay, which does have a Time knob. It looks carried over rather than written for this pedal.

Ins, outs and power

Mono in / mono out uses the top set of jacks; mono in / stereo out and stereo in / stereo out are both supported (sheet 7). MIDI In and MIDI Thru. Power is 9V DC centre-negative, 300 mA minimum: “The use of an isolated power supply is recommended for powering all Walrus Audio Pedals. Daisy chain power supplies are not recommended.” (sheet 3)

MIDI CC map

The firmware 2.0 list. Program change 0–8 recalls the nine onboard presets in bank order; 9–127 are the MIDI-only slots.

Which M1 is which

Three manuals cover three different M1s. This page follows the firmware 2.0 manual. Listed below are the differences that change what a control does, or remove it.

Original M1, launch firmware

Same hardware · earlier manual
  • Chorus types differ. Type 2 was Series Chorus, which the launch-firmware manual describes as “essentially running two chorus pedals in series”, and Type 3 was Tri-Chorus. Firmware 2.0 moved Tri-Chorus to Type 2 and put a Flanger on Type 3, leaving no slot for Series Chorus.
  • Rotary X was Cab Simulation: “Controls the mix of a physical rotary cabinet model into your signal.” Sheet 16 of the 2.0 manual lists it as “X: Not used.”
  • Filter was laid out differently. Tone set the filter's centre frequency, X set its resonance, and the launch-firmware manual told you to “Drive the filter frequency with the main LFO”. Firmware 2.0 puts cutoff on Rate, envelope amount on Depth, resonance on Tone, filter attack on Sym and filter release on X.

Original M1, firmware 2.0

This page
  • Six programs, three types each, Prog knob and three toggles.
  • Tap divisions: quarter, quarter-note triplet, eighth.
  • Nine onboard presets, 128 over MIDI, no preset copy or swap.

M1 MKII

Different hardware · separate manual
  • Three encoders and a screen replace the Tweak knob, Prog knob, Tune knob and all three toggles. Rate, Depth and Lo-Fi stay as knobs.
  • Five tap divisions, adding dotted eighth and sixteenth, plus a BPM parameter you can set numerically.
  • Preset copy, paste, swap and overwrite from the preset menu.
  • Analog dry mix and a USB-C port for firmware and IR loading.
  • Rotary X becomes MIC, adjusting virtual mic placement. Chorus Type 3 flanger gains its own feedback control. Bypass modes are renamed Relay and Buffered.

All three manuals are in this repo: original · firmware 2.0 · MKII.
