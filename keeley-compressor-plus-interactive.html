What the pedal is doing

Effect engaged

How Blend mixes the two signals

A compressor turns down whatever is louder than its threshold. The loudest moment in a guitar note is the pick attack at the front of it, so a compressor turns that down too. Blend mixes uncompressed signal back in alongside the compressed signal: control 2 on the sheet “adjusts mix of compressed and uncompressed signal. Full left for clean, full right for full compression.” Keeley's product page gives the idea a name — “The Blend Control is a way of getting the peaks to come through while having sustain at the same time. So if you want sustain more than peak-limiting, this style of Manhattan Compression is for you!”

Keeley print the two ends of the sweep on the enclosure, CLEAN and FULL, and nothing in between. The percentages here are this page's arithmetic off the knob angle; the pedal's real taper is not published.

This drawing is by this page. Keeley print no signal-flow diagram anywhere. The split into two paths follows from Blend mixing “compressed and uncompressed signal.” Level is drawn after the mix because Keeley say it “adjusts for gain lost to compression or blend” — to make up for the blend it has to sit downstream of it. Tone is left out of the drawing because nothing Keeley publish says where in the chain it sits.

The two ends of the sweep

Fully left. Control 2: “full left for clean.” No compressed signal reaches the output; the pedal passes what you played, with Level and Tone on it.

Fully right. Control 2: “full right for full compression.” No uncompressed signal reaches the output, so the pick attack is compressed along with the rest of the note. The sheet does not say that last part — it follows from the uncompressed signal being mixed all the way down.

Sustain and Blend

Sustain “adjusts compression ratio and increases sustain” — control 1. Blend then decides how much of that compressed signal you hear at the output.

Turn Sustain up and leave Blend short of full: the long tail comes from the compressed signal while the pick attack still arrives through the uncompressed one. That is what Keeley mean by getting “the peaks to come through while having sustain at the same time.” Their note on control 1 holds at any Blend setting — “higher sustain settings will create noise when not playing.”

The Single Coil / Humbucker switch

Control 3 on the sheet is Attack / Release, and it “adjusts the attack and release for single coils or humbuckers.” Attack is how quickly the compressor clamps down once the signal crosses its threshold; release is how quickly it lets go once the signal falls back under. The switch sets both at once, and it is the only way to change either.

Single coil — switch up

Keeley describe what the switch is for, not what this position does: “The new Release Switch on the Keeley Compressor Plus is designed to make it very easy to transition between single coil and humbucker guitars.” No attack or release time is published for either position, and the only comparison Keeley draw is that the humbucker side releases quickly.

Humbucker — switch down

Keeley, on their product page: “Humbuckers have more average energy than single coils which can cause compressors to stay in ‘compression mode’ for a lot longer. When this happens, any series of notes you play will sound dull. With the quick release of the new Compressor Plus, your humbuckers remain true sounding, and each note retains dynamic attack and definition.”

Try the other position too

Keeley's labels say which position usually suits which pickup, and stop there. A single coil played hard through fast runs may still sound better on the humbucker side, and a humbucker picked lightly on the single coil side — that is this page's suggestion, not Keeley's.

Where Tone came from

Keeley, on their product page: “The Tone Control is our own design taken from analog delays and other vintage effects. Those effects used an emphasis circuit to recover treble and highs after they went through the BBD or other analog delay lines. We took that circuit and made it into a full fledged tone control! We have found that it is the perfect way to gently add back in those highs lost in the compression process.”

The sheet gives one instruction for using it — “turn right for brighter tone” — and marks no flat position. To find one, switch the pedal off and on and match Tone to the bypassed sound.

Finding unity on Level

Control 4 “adjusts for gain lost to compression or blend. Set to unity or turn it up for a boost.” Keeley print no knob position for unity, and where it falls moves with Sustain and Blend, since those are the two controls the gain is being lost to. Switch the pedal off and on and match it by ear.

Keeley on where the boost is worth using: “Use with your favorite fuzz to create a wall of tone like you've only heard on records. Looping has never been easier than with this compressor.” And on amps: “Nothing pushes an amp better than the Keeley Compressor Plus.”

The three settings on the sheet

Under EXAMPLE SETTINGS the sheet prints three small pictures of the pedal with its knobs drawn in position. It prints no numbers and no clock positions, so the positions below were measured off those pictures by eye and are close rather than exact. Pressing one sets the pedal above.

What changes between the three

Sustain, Blend and the switch. Level and Tone are drawn at noon in all three pictures, and Sustain covers the widest range of the three — roughly 9:00 on Always On to 3:00 on Nashville Lead.

Power and connections

Power

From the sheet: “9VDC center negative, 10mA+ power supply or 9V battery.” Keeley's product page gives the draw as 10mA.

In and out

From the sheet: “mono instrument input, mono instrument output.” The product page lists the pedal as “True-Bypass,” which the sheet does not mention.

Circuit

“100% Analog – Made Right Here.” Keeley: “The Keeley Compressor Plus is a compressor with an expander/sustainer. Not only does it act as a regular compressor, limiting the peaks in your playing, it adds more and more gain as notes fade out.”
