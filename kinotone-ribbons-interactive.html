Knobs and pages

Ribbons has four knobs in a square and four pages of effects. The push button steps through the pages, and each knob does a different job on each page — so four knobs cover sixteen parameters. Everything else about the pedal follows from that one idea.

The labels above each knob tell you its four jobs

Four abbreviations are printed above every knob, listed in page order. LP · WD · DD · RM above the top-left knob means Low-pass on page 1, Wow depth on page 2, Dropouts depth on page 3, Reverb mix on page 4.

On the pedal above, the abbreviation for the current page is printed brighter than the other three, and the full parameter name is spelled out under each knob. Change page and both follow. The full names are this page's addition — your enclosure has only the abbreviations.

The LED strip is the display

There is no screen. The four LEDs below the knobs are the readout. Their colour tells you the page — green, blue, pink or red, matching the four icons printed above them. Each LED's brightness tells you that knob's value: dark at 0%, some white at 12 noon, bright white at 100%.

The push button sits just to the right of the strip.

Everything reads clockwise from the bottom-left

The LEDs run left to right in the order bottom-left, top-left, top-right, bottom-right, and the manual lists every page's parameters in that same order. When someone hands you a setting, that is the sequence they mean.

The knobs are relative

Because one knob serves four parameters, its physical position cannot match all of them. After a page change, turning a knob does nothing until its position sweeps past the stored value and picks it up.

Change pages on the pedal above and a marker appears beside each knob showing where its stored value sits. Sweep the knob to the marker and the two connect.

Three knobs that don't work the way you'd expect

Low-pass (top-left, page 1) opens clockwise, and fully counter-clockwise mutes the pedal. High-pass (bottom-left, page 1) opens counter-clockwise, and fully clockwise mutes the pedal. Reverb mix & placement (top-left, page 4) is 0% at 12 noon and increases in both directions.

About the two filters

Both are slightly resonant four-pole virtual-analog designs modelled on the filter from a well-known 1970s synthesiser, and they are symmetrical opposites of each other. Sculpting harmonics with them is most of what makes a lo-fi sound convincing — more so than any amount of noise or wobble.

Touch modes

The Touch footswitch triggers one effect of your choosing, and there are seven to choose from. Changing mode is a gesture rather than a knob: hold the push button and tap Touch. The selected mode's two parameters live on the right-hand knobs of page 4, and the Touch LED tells you which mode is loaded by colour, solid or flickering.

Choosing a mode below sets it on the pedal and jumps to page 4 so you can see its two parameters.

Magnetic Dance: the Rate knob selects a chord

Magnetic Dance captures a micro-loop of up to 20 seconds and runs four stereo tape heads across it, each jumping to a random start point when it reaches the end. Rate (top-right on page 4) tunes those playback speeds to musical intervals, so the knob works as a list of chords read clockwise from fully counter-clockwise.

The shaded quadrant is the forgiving one. Unity, oct and 5 sit in the first quarter of the sweep and work on almost any material. The rest want single notes, or chords built from octaves, fourths or fifths.

The other knob, Direction & Volume (bottom-right), is loudest at 12 noon. Left of noon plays in reverse, right of noon plays forward, and the volume falls away toward both extremes.

The looper

Ribbons also contains a four-track looper. Hold the push button and tap Bypass to enter it, and the Bypass LED turns blue. The push button now cycles tracks instead of pages, and the four knobs become per-track controls. Each track starts at normal speed, continuous scale, forward, full volume.

Speed and speed scale

12 noon is 1×, which gives a 20-second loop. Fully counter-clockwise is roughly 0.5× — 40 seconds, an octave down. Fully clockwise is roughly 2× — 10 seconds, an octave up.

Speed scale (bottom-right) quantises what Speed does, clockwise from fully counter-clockwise: continuous, chromatic, fourths at noon, fifths, octaves.

Volume and direction

Set a track's volume to zero before recording and you can swell the loop in by hand. Turning a track fully down stops it without erasing it.

Direction flips a track to reverse. Overdubs recorded after the flip play forward while the original stays reversed.

Setup and hidden options

Hidden options

Hold Bypass while adjusting controls, on any page, to reach filter resonance, makeup gain (±6dB), and the compression-to-saturation swap. All of it saves to presets, and the swap stays until you change it back.

Input level

Hold Touch at power-on to choose stereo or mono in, at instrument or line level. Units from 2022–23 upgraded to v2.0 have no line-level option, since instrument level carries the headroom.

Bypass modes

Hold Bypass at power-on to choose analog bypass, buffered and silent, or buffered with trails so reverb tails and loops keep ringing.

Power and audio

9VDC centre-negative, 180mA minimum, isolated supply. TRS stereo in and out, with mono TS supported. CV and expression take 0–5V, where the knob position is the 2.5V midpoint.

Presets

Eight onboard and 32 over MIDI. Hold the push button to open the menu, tap to move through the slots — red is 1–4, blue is 5–8 — then tap Touch to load or tap Bypass twice to save. Slot 1 is INIT and cannot be overwritten.

MIDI, firmware 2.0

CC 14–27 are the tape effects, 28–29 the Touch parameters, 30 the Touch mode, 31 switches effects and looper, 32–50 the looper tracks, 57–59 the hidden options. Program Change loads presets 1–32. A four-voice sine synth responds to note data and runs through everything.
