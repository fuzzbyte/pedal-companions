PLUS PedalSustain unit for melodic instruments

Printed page 2: “The PLUS pedal is a new kind of audio processing engine that offers piano-style sustain/sostenuto effects for electric guitars and all other melodic instruments.” It records your instrument the whole time it is on. Press the treadle and it takes the ringing part of the note or chord you just played and loops it.

Gamechanger asks

“For best results we ask you to place the PLUS Pedal FIRST in your signal chain.” Printed page 2.

And requires

“Use only power supplies that can produce at least 200mA of current at 9V DC (center negative 5.5 x 2.1mm barrel connector).” Printed page 16, repeating the requirement given on printed page 2.

How it makes the sound

pg. 2

Printed page 2: “The PLUS Pedal does not use oscillators or filters to synthesize sounds! Instead – it produces sound using the real signal from your instrument.” Printed page 3 names the method: “This method of sound synthesis is called Real Time Sampling and it was developed specifically for PLUS Pedal by Gamechanger Audio.”

1 · It is always recording

Printed page 2: “When the PLUS is on – it constantly records all audio signal coming from your instrument”.

2 · The press picks an event

Printed page 2: “as soon as you press the foot-pedal it is able to immediately spring into action and start analyzing the most recent audio event – such as a strummed chord, or a separate note on a wind instrument.”

3 · The ringing becomes a loop

Printed page 2: “The PLUS Pedal will now proceed to create a ‘usable loop’ out of the ringing part of the chord. Think of it as a ‘Smart Looper’ that only catches the second half of each note or chord you play and samples that short snippet of sound into a seamless, continuous loop.”

What the sampling keeps

Printed page 3: “‘Real Time Audio Sampling’ ensures that each sustained note or chord will retain the exact character of your instrument and faithfully reflect the dynamics of your playing style. It also makes PLUS Pedal compatible with any type of melodic instruments, such as brass or woodwinds, all string instruments, and even vocals.”

The same page on playing technique: “Real Time Sampling is also responsive to various playing styles – create continuous layers of sustain incorporating vibrato, bends, harmonics, trills and other dynamic playing techniques. You can also use your whammy bar, play with a slide or experiment with amp feedback or noises – the PLUS Pedal will capture all of these tonal nuances and turn them into a continuous sound.”

The gap between the attack and the press

pg. 3

Printed page 3: “The quality and resolution of the WET signal produced by the PLUS Pedal will always be determined by the amount of time between the attack of the chord/note and the pedal being pressed down.” Drag the marker to move the press.

The shaded block is the part of the ring-out the pedal turns into a loop; the outlined tiles to its right are that snippet repeating. The envelope shape and the marker's position. Printed page 3 gives no times, only the two ends of the range — quick after the attack, or a longer interval — so the drawing here shows the mechanism rather than measured values.

What is simulated here and what is not

The slot behaviour above follows the manual: five slots, rotational replacement, the layer cap set by SUSTAIN in Group mode, and what a full tap and a half tap each remove.

The fade-in and fade-out times. Printed page 6 describes RISE and TAIL in words and knob positions and never gives a number of seconds, so the times animated here were picked to be legible on screen. Do not read them as measurements of the pedal.

GRADUAL control

pg. 4

Printed page 4, under the heading GRADUAL control: “The foot-pedal does not always have to be pressed all the way - there is a distinct middle position, which requires only a light touch.”

Half press

Printed page 4: “In the middle-press mode, the sustained tones produced by the PLUS Pedal will be slightly muffled and lower volume.”

Gamechanger, on the same page: “We encourage you to go back and forth between a half-press and a full press to adjust the volume and tone of each sustained layer on the fly.”

On the short tap: “A short half-press of the foot pedal will only remove the last recorded layer of sustain.”

Full press

Printed page 3: “Each note will be sustained for as long as the pedal is pressed down, and once you let go of the pedal, the sustained note/chord will start decaying according to the value of the ‘TAIL’ setting”.

Printed page 4, on the short tap: “A short Full tap of the foot-pedal will act as a kill switch and remove all layers of sustain currently active.”

The LED

Printed page 4: “This middle position is indicated by the PLUS Pedal’s main LED – you will see that it has 3 levels of brightness: off (standby), half, and full.” The features list on printed page 5 calls it “Indication LED (two brightness stages)”.

Page 4 counts three because it counts the off state; page 5 counts two because it counts only the lit ones.

One press per sound

Printed page 4: “Depending on the value of the TAIL knob and other settings, the PLUS Pedal allows you to stack and sustain up to 5 sounds, but bear in mind that each new note or chord you’d like to sustain will require a separate foot-pedal input.”

Single mode and Group mode

pg. 7–8

A two-position slide switch on the side. Printed page 5 lists it as “1 two-position slide switch, labeled ‘SINGLE and GROUP’: Lets you go between two distinct modes of playing”. Printed page 8: “SUSTAIN is a special knob that changes its functionality when you switch from Single mode to Group mode.”

Holding the treadleWhat SUSTAIN does
SINGLE Printed page 7: “holding down the foot-pedal will allow you to sustain one (most recent) note/chord. All previous audio slots will continue fading out according to the speed set by the TAIL knob.” Printed page 8: “the SUSTAIN knob allows you to control the behavior of the hold-function (while the foot-pedal is pressed down). When set to minimum, it will emulate the natural decaying properties of a ringing string – gradual reduction of high frequencies.” At the ∞ mark it “will keep the sustained note/chord completely static for as long as the foot-pedal is pressed down, without applying any frequency filters.”
GROUP Printed page 7: “pressing and holding the foot-pedal lets you hold the whole group of audio layers currently active. For example – you can use a medium TAIL Setting to create a quick three note arpeggio and then just continue holding all three layers by keeping the foot-pedal pressed down.” Printed page 8: “the SUSTAIN knob allows you to reduce the number of layers allowed simultaneously – you can choose from 1 to 5 layers”. The count shows on the LED: “This is indicated by the main LED, which will produce a single flash when the SUSTAIN knob is turned all the way down. If you’d like to use two circular layers, turn the SUSTAIN knob to around 10 o’clock until you see two quick flashes, and so on.”

The tone setting carries over

The footnote on printed page 8: “Any tone and dynamics settings achieved with the SUSTAIN knob in the SINGLE mode will be applied to all layers in the GROUP mode.”

Filling all five slots

Printed page 7: “When all 5 slots are filled up – each new slot will be blended in on a rotational basis – slot number 6 will replace the 1st slot; slot 7 replaces the 2nd slot, and so on.”

The manual gives two positions on the SUSTAIN sweep: minimum for one layer, around 10 o'clock for two. It does not say where three, four and five sit. This page splits the sweep into five equal bands, which puts two at around 10 o'clock. Count the LED flashes on your own pedal to see where the bands actually fall.

Outputs, SPLIT and MIX

pg. 9–10

Printed page 9: “The PLUS Pedal features two ¼ inch output jacks labeled ‘OUT’ and ‘CLEAN OUT/FSW’. There is also a toggle switch on the Pedal’s side, labeled SPLIT & MIX.” Flip that switch on the pedal above, or with the buttons here.

MIX

With the switch at MIX, OUT sends, from printed page 9: “the combined DRY signal coming from your instrument with the WET signal, this is the PLUS Pedal’s most commonly used mode and it can be activated by setting the above-mentioned toggle switch to ‘MIX’”.

SPLIT — “WET only”

With the switch at SPLIT, OUT sends, from printed page 9: “only the isolated WET signal generated by the PLUS Pedal. This way your instrument’s DRY signal will be muted, but you will still be able to produce sustained tones as before, by using the foot-pedal.”

The same page: “This mode of playing is called ‘WET only’ – and it comes in very handy when creating soundscapes, cinematic ambiance and other unworldly sound effects. The WET only mode can be activated by positioning the toggle switch to ‘SPLIT’.”

Two outputs at once

Printed page 10: “The second output, labeled ‘CLEAN OUT/FSW’ sends out the unaffected Dry signal from your instrument at all times. Thanks to this dual output system the PLUS Pedal offers a lot of diversity when it comes to setting up your signal chain and creating stereo effects.”

The diagram on that page: “Use two amps for a stereo feel; In a studio setting - it can be useful to keep a separate DRY signal going into a mixing board or recording software at all times.”

The WET Only Footswitch

Printed page 11: “The WET Only Footswitch serves as a remote foot-controller that bypasses the SPLIT & MIX toggle switch on the PLUS Pedal’s side panel, thus letting you switch in and out of the WET Only mode on the fly.”

“(Note: Switch for OUT 1 must be set to MIX in order to use the footswitch.)” The same page adds that “you can still access the DRY only signal through a special DRY OUT output built into the Footswitch itself.” pg. 11

The FX loop and other instruments

pg. 12–13

SEND and RETURN

Printed page 13: “PLUS Pedal features a very easily accessible FX Loop that allows you to channel the isolated WET signal through other external effects units. The FX Loop does not have a separate on/off toggle switch – simply connect any effects device to the PLUS Pedal via the SEND and RETURN jacks and switch the external effect on. Now any WET signal produced by the PLUS Pedal will be channeled through this external effect.”

Gamechanger's suggestions on the same page: “connect a Tremolo or Vibrato unit to add a satisfying pulsation to all of the sustained notes / chords”; “Modulation effects, such as Choruses, Flangers or Phaser will add a nice psychedelic ‘turbulence’ to your sustained layers”; “Pitch shifters, Harmonizers and Octavers are great for creating complex tonal content, polyphonic arrangements and songwriting.”

Microphones

Printed page 12: “PLUS Pedal is compatible with all types of microphones, but it does not have a dedicated XLR input. For best results use a pre-amp to run the instrument’s signal into the PLUS Pedal via a 1/4 inch audio jack cable. To achieve a smooth tone, always make sure that the gain level of the input signal going into the PLUS Pedal is not clipping”. Printed page 15 gives the ceiling: max input level +6.8 dBu.

The footnote on page 12: “when using loud acoustic instruments in small rooms try setting the PLUS Pedal’s SPLIT - MIX switch to ‘SPLIT’ and running a second channel for the DRY signal. This way you can adjust the WET / DRY balance more accurately on the mixing desk”.

Which PLUS this manual covers

Original PLUS Pedal

This is the manual for the original PLUS Pedal.

Gamechanger also sell a PLUS Pedal II, and nothing in this document is written for it. The document carries no revision number, no date and no “Mk I” anywhere in it — it simply says PLUS PEDAL throughout, so nothing on the cover tells a reader holding a PLUS Pedal II that this is the wrong document. The identifiers are inside: the specification table on printed page 15 gives a 26 kHz sample rate at 16 bits, 200 mA draw and a 90 × 195 × 72 mm enclosure, and the panel it describes has the SINGLE/GROUP and SPLIT/MIX slide switches and a CLEAN OUT/FSW jack. If your unit does not match that list, check Gamechanger's current manual rather than this one.
